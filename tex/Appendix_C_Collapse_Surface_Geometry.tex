\section{Appendix C: Collapse-Surface Geometry}
\label{appendix:collapsesurface}

\subsection{C.1 Motivation}

Collapse surfaces arise when geometric transport interacts with curvature,
slack dimensions, and projection thresholds. In classical softmax-based
systems, collapse surfaces are uncontrolled and erase origin, holonomy, and
spectral structure. Under the FSFE manifold, collapse surfaces become
well-defined geometric objects with stable boundaries and invariant transport.

\subsection{C.2 Collapse Manifold Definition}

Let $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{FSFE}} = (M, g, \nabla, \kappa, \sigma)$ be the FSFE
manifold. A \emph{collapse surface} is a submanifold
\[
C_k \subset M
\]
defined by a curvature-bound threshold:
\[
C_k = \{ v \in M \mid \kappa(v) = k \}.
\]

Collapse surfaces partition $M$ into curvature-level sets.

\paragraph{Slack Buffering.}
For slack coordinates $s \in \mathbb{R}^k$:
\[
v \in C_k \Rightarrow v + s \in C_k.
\]

Slack ensures collapse surfaces do not erase origin.

\paragraph{Holonomy Invariance.}
For any loop $\gamma$:
\[
\mathrm{Hol}_\gamma(C_k) = C_k.
\]

\subsection{C.3 Collapse Boundary Conditions}

Define the collapse boundary:
\[
\partial C_k = \{ v \in M \mid \kappa(v) = k, \; \nabla \kappa(v) \neq 0 \}.
\]

Boundary conditions must satisfy:

\begin{itemize}
    \item curvature monotonicity,
    \item slack stability,
    \item holonomy invariance,
    \item spectral boundedness.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Curvature Monotonicity.}
\[
\kappa(v) \le \kappa(w) \Rightarrow C_{\kappa(v)} \subseteq C_{\kappa(w)}.
\]

\paragraph{Spectral Stability.}
\[
\sigma(v) \le \sigma(w) \Rightarrow \partial C_{\kappa(v)} \subseteq \partial C_{\kappa(w)}.
\]

\subsection{C.4 Collapse Transport}

Transport along collapse surfaces is defined by:
\[
T_C(v) = P_\gamma(v)
\]
where $P_\gamma$ is parallel transport restricted to $C_k$.

\paragraph{Metric Preservation.}
\[
g(T_C(v), T_C(v)) = g(v,v).
\]

\paragraph{Curvature Preservation.}
\[
\kappa(T_C(v)) = \kappa(v).
\]

\paragraph{Slack Preservation.}
\[
T_C(v + s) = T_C(v) + s.
\]

\subsection{C.5 Collapse-Safe Projection}

Given the holonomy-safe projection operator $\Pi$ from Appendix B, define the
collapse-safe projection:
\[
\Pi_C(v) = \Pi(v) \quad \text{for } v \in C_k.
\]

Collapse-safe projection satisfies:

\[
\Pi_C(T_C(v)) = \Pi_C(v).
\]

Thus projection is stable under collapse transport.

\subsection{C.6 Collapse-Surface Invariants}

Define the collapse invariant set:
\[
I_C(v) = \{ g(v,v), \kappa(v), \sigma(v) \}.
\]

Collapse surfaces preserve invariants:
\[
I_C(T_C(v)) = I_C(v).
\]

These invariants are required for spectral anti-drift analysis in Appendix D.
